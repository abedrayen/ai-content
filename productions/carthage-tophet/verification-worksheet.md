## c22 · interpretation · attributed to: Schwartz et al. 2010, 2012, 2017
**Claim:** Schwartz and colleagues found most individuals were between late fetal and about six months old, with many prenatal; they argue this matches natural perinatal mortality and that tophets were cemeteries for those who died shortly before or after birth, whatever the cause, while acknowledging the Carthaginians may occasionally have sacrificed humans.

**Review status:** verified-by-source-review

**Evidence:** S1 abstract (read in full): most 'prenatal to 5-to-6 postnatal months, with a significant presence of prenates'; 'consistent with modern-day data on perinatal mortality'; 'Tophets were cemeteries for those who died shortly before or after birth, regardless of the cause'; framed as compatible with Carthaginians having 'occasionally sacrificed humans'.
