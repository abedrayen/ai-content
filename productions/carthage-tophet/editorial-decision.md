# Editorial decision: YouTube Short (carthage-tophet)

Status: **approved by the editor (in chat), 2026-11-17.** The angle, fairness rules and script were approved. Visual and audio production has not started. This is the companion Short agreed in decision 2 of `editorial-proposal.md`. The long-form piece remains the primary production.

## Selected angle
**"Same bones, conflicting verdicts."**
Two specialist teams examined largely the same collection of cremated infant remains from Carthage's tophet and reached conflicting conclusions about how the children died. The Short explains what was found, what each team concluded and why their methods differ. It ends on the question that remains open.

## Why this angle
- **It is genuinely true and surprising.** The hook is a verified fact (c21), not a provocation.
- **The mystery is how we know, not the horror.** That is the brand's "mystery without misinformation" principle in action.
- **It forces fairness.** The structure gives each interpretation its own attributed segment of roughly equal length.
- **It differs from the long-form.** The long-form opens on the 1921 discovery. The Short opens on the scientific dispute, so the two complement rather than repeat each other.
- **It relies only on claims that are audited ✅ or ✏️.** It needs none that require further research, apart from the location text in c01.

## Angles considered and rejected for the Short
| Angle | Reason not chosen |
|---|---|
| The 1921 moonlight discovery | A strong hook, but in 45–60 s it leaves no room for the evidence or the debate. Reserved for the long-form cold open and a possible second Short. |
| "What the ancient writers claimed" | Leads with Diodorus's most graphic imagery, which risks sensationalism and platform restrictions. |
| Amulets: "children given protective charms" | Emotionally powerful, but on its own it leans toward one interpretation. |
| Zita: "sick children buried with care" | About a different site after Carthage's fall, so it could mislead about Carthage in a short format. |

## Fairness rules for the script
1. **Attribute every interpretation by name** in narration and on screen ("Patricia Smith's team…", "Jeffrey Schwartz's team…"). Never say "scientists found".
2. **Give the two segments equal length** (about 8–9 s each).
3. **Include the concession.** Schwartz's team accepts occasional sacrifice may have happened (S1). Leaving this out would misrepresent their position as outright denial.
4. **Order:** Smith's view first, then Schwartz's. To keep the second view from seeming to have the last word, the next segment turns to method, which applies to *both* teams, and the Short closes on the open question.
5. **No consensus claims** in either direction (audit c29, c32). "Scholars still disagree" is supported. "Most experts" and "experts are split evenly" are not.
6. **Write about the texts, not the events.** "Greek writers accused…", not "Carthaginians did…".

## Sensitivity and platform rules
- No images of human remains, no reconstructions of any killing, and no infant sound effects.
- The thumbnail and title must name the scientific mystery, not death. Suggested title: **"Same Bones, Two Verdicts: The Carthage Tophet Mystery"**.
- Use calm, clinical narration. Avoid words like "horrifying", "dark secret" or "shocking".

## Duration
A target of about 55 s. The narration draft is 145 words, which is about 55–58 s at a measured documentary pace. A timed read-through is required. If it runs long, trim segment 2 first.
