# Gate 1: Topic validation

- **Working title:** *Carthage's Tophet: Burial Ground or Place of Sacrifice?* (long-form). The companion Short is *Same Bones, Two Verdicts*.
- **Format:** **long**, about 8.5–9 minutes, 16:9 (primary), plus a **short** of 56 s, 9:16 (companion; script approved 2026-11-17).
- **Pillar:** ancient-civilizations (archaeology). It also serves the brand's North African and Mediterranean history commitment.
- **Central question (one sentence):** What do the archaeological remains from Carthage's tophet actually tell us about the fate of the infants buried there, and can the evidence establish whether ritual sacrifice took place?
- **Editorial angle:** *Same bones, two verdicts.* The mystery is how we know. The film follows the evidence:
  1. place;
  2. discovery;
  3. ancient accusation;
  4. inscriptions;
  5. the skeletal science;
  6. the disagreement;
  7. the limits.

  It ends on the question that remains open. Interpretations are always credited to the scholars who hold them (the pipeline's `interpretation` label).
- **Target audience:** English-speaking adults aged 18–40 who like archaeology, ancient history and science documentaries. They are curious and sceptical of sensationalism, and are mostly watching on mobile (the Short) and desktop or TV (the long-form).
- **Narrative structure (long-form):**
  1. Cold open
  2. Carthage
  3. The discovery
  4. The accusation
  5. Voices in stone
  6. The scientific investigation
  7. Two verdicts
  8. Beyond Carthage
  9. What we can and cannot know
  10. Closing question

  See `script-long.md`.
- **Why this story / what is distinctive:** A North African heritage site at the centre of a real, unresolved scientific dispute. Two specialist teams examined largely the same cremated remains and reached conflicting conclusions. Most popular coverage picks a side. We show how the evidence is weighed, and include Tunisian scholarship (Fantar; the 2014–18 Tunisian excavations).
- **Is there enough credible material?** Yes:
  - peer-reviewed papers on both sides (Schwartz et al. 2010, 2012, 2017; Smith et al. 2011, 2013; Xella et al. 2013);
  - the excavator's own account (Stager 2014);
  - an independent 2024 review (McCarty, *Bryn Mawr Classical Review*);
  - a 2024 open-access comparison study from Zita (Cerezo-Román et al.);
  - the primary ancient texts (Diodorus 20.14; Plutarch, *On Superstition* 13).

  See `sources.md`. **Limitation:** several key papers were read only in abstract or extract form, and this is recorded per claim in `claims.json` and `claim-audit.md`.
- **Risk of misinformation / sensitivity:** high.
  - **The subject:** it concerns the deaths of infants and an accusation of child sacrifice.
  - **Mitigations:**
    - each interpretation is credited to the scholars who hold it;
    - no claims of consensus in either direction;
    - no graphic imagery, no human remains on screen, no reconstruction of any killing;
    - ancient accounts are presented as texts, not events;
    - the 20,000-urn extrapolation is excluded;
    - the language is calm and clinical;
    - the thumbnail and title frame the scientific mystery.
  - **Advertiser-friendly review:** see `youtube-package.md`.
- **Companion piece:** the 56 s Short, "Same Bones, Two Verdicts". It has its own structure: a hook on the dispute, then evidence, then both readings, then method, then the open question. It is not a cut-down of the long-form.
